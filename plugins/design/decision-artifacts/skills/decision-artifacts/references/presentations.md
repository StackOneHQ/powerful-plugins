# Presentations

Use a deck when the decision will be walked through live: a review, a steering meeting, an all-hands. The decision spine and evidence model stay the same; only the disclosure changes. A slide is the at-rest layer, and the appendix, speaker notes and linked artifact are the on-demand layer.

## Surface

- In Claude, prefer the Slides template (`/slides` with a brief in Claude Code). People can edit any slide directly, comment and ask Claude to revise, present from the browser, and export to formats such as PowerPoint or PDF. Confirm the current export options in the account before promising one.
- When there is no native slide surface, build one self-contained HTML page with one section per slide, keyboard navigation (arrow keys, Home, End), a visible slide counter and a print stylesheet that puts one slide per page.
- When the organisation presents from an existing slide tool, follow its template and export into it rather than recreating its look.

## Spine to slides

Keep the main deck short enough to present in the time available, usually five to eight slides:

1. **Decision slide:** the recommendation or current state, why now, and the ask of this audience. It should stand alone if the meeting ends after it.
2. **Primary visual:** the comparison, before/after, flow or status view that carries the argument, with its takeaway as the slide title.
3. **Alternatives and trade-offs:** only the criteria that change the decision.
4. **Risks and unknowns:** what would change the recommendation, with confidence in words.
5. **Next action:** owner, date and how the result will be verified.

Move every supporting evidence record into appendix slides or the speaker notes, ordered to match the main slides, and link the full interactive artifact when one exists. Nothing consequential is dropped because it did not fit on a slide.

## Slide rules

- Write each slide title as the claim, not the topic: "Option B halves review time", not "Results".
- One idea and at most one primary visual per slide. Label values directly.
- Keep body text short enough to read from the back of the room; detail goes in notes or the appendix.
- Show sources and last-checked dates on the slide for volatile numbers, in small type.
- Keep a text equivalent for every chart in the notes or the appendix.
- Give each slide a stable title so comments and revision requests can point at it.

## Review before presenting

Read the deck end to end in presenter view or at presentation size. Check that the decision slide alone states the ask, that each main slide's title matches its visual, that no number differs between a slide and its appendix record, and that exported files keep fonts, charts and notes. Sharing the deck or sending an export to other people follows the same confirmation rule as publishing an artifact.
